## The Context

I joined Yieldstreet in 2017 as a software engineer (employee #13), when the team could fit in a single room. Since then I have moved through engineering, product management, senior product management, and into my current Director role at what is now Willow Wealth. The platform sits in alternative investments and private markets, an environment governed by SEC and FINRA requirements, where compliance shapes product design from the first sketch.

This is a look at the product decisions I made and learned from along the way.

## Phase 1: Engineering the Foundation (2017–2018)

Joining as an engineer gave me a deep understanding of the technical constraints and possibilities of the platform. I worked on:

- **Core investment flows**: the transactional backbone for investor onboarding, accreditation, and fund allocation
- **Compliance automation**: automating what we could of the regulatory overhead to reduce friction for both operations and investors
- **Data pipelines**: the analytics infrastructure that later informed product decisions

The best product decisions come from people who understand what the system can and cannot do.

## Phase 2: The Product Pivot (2018–2021)

I moved into product because I saw a gap: strong engineering, but a need for someone to translate investor needs into technical requirements while keeping the architecture scalable.

- **Greenfield product launches**: multiple new investment verticals from concept to launch, each with its own regulatory, UX, and technical requirements
- **Platform unification**: a unified investor experience across previously siloed products as asset classes were added
- **First 0-to-1 product**: a new savings account product that scaled to 19K accounts and $100M+ in deposits within six months of launch
- **Retirement accounts**: launched the IRA product with our custodian, Pensco Trust, at a time when few custodians offered APIs or accepted e-signatures instead of notarized paper. Setup took 31 days at launch; we iterated it to 7 and eliminated 1,200+ hours a year of manual work. Added a partnership with Capitalize for 401(k)-to-IRA rollovers
- **Self-service tooling**: an internal no-code admin platform that let marketing and product teams build audience segments, run A/B tests, and automate campaigns without engineering tickets

### The Lesson: Build for the Partner's Worst Day

In 2020 the custodian's planned two-day weekend migration turned into a month-long outage, and we weren't ready for it. I escalated to the custodian's CEO; onsite meetings with both CEOs produced the partnership's first SLAs and SLOs. On our side we built retries, audits, and other resilience features so the platform could absorb the next disruption. The IRA business recovered and grew to roughly $75M in assets.

### The Second Lesson: Go Where the Workarounds Are

The most impactful improvement of this period came from spending a week shadowing the investor relations team. Their workarounds and manual processes revealed platform gaps that no amount of analytics would have surfaced.

## Phase 3: Scaling Product Leadership (2021–2025)

As Senior PM and then Director (managing three PMs), the work shifted from building features to building systems:

- **Next custodian**: moved retirement custody to Equity Trust in 2023, integrating its WealthBridge account link so Equity Trust clients could invest directly
- **Discovery as a practice**: established the product discovery process and ran the first user research, which led to a dedicated UX research team
- **Audit readiness**: co-led the SOC 2 and ISO 27001 programs, supplied control evidence to auditors, and ran vendor security reviews against SOC 2/ISO reports and trust centers

- **Platform thinking**: moving the team's mental model from features to reusable capabilities that serve multiple products
- **Widening the product surface**: broadening from alternative investments into wider private markets meant rethinking information architecture, redesigning onboarding for different risk profiles, and building compliance workflows for additional asset types
- **Cross-functional alignment**: with engineering, design, and a growing marketing organization, coordination became the product challenge

### Regulated Launches and Partnerships

The most recent product work is where regulation, partnerships, and distribution strategy came together:

- **Willow 360 managed portfolios** (formerly Yieldstreet 360): $10M+ in assets under management, with allocations advised by Wilshire
- **Registered funds**: $6M in AUM within two months of launch
- **Atomic integration**: embedded brokerage for trade execution and reconciliation, running dual-tenant RIA and broker-dealer programs simultaneously
- **Distribution model**: co-drove a strategic shift in distribution with the company's founder. This was a shared strategic effort, not a solo call

## Phase 4: Applied AI (2026–Present)

The fourth phase started the way the others did — with a gap nobody owned. As the company deployed AI across every function, the hard questions weren't about models: who decides what an AI system is allowed to do, what "good enough to ship" means when the author is an agent, and how to measure adoption and value without fooling yourself.

That's the job now: AI enablement, governance, and infrastructure across the organization, as the company's Claude Enterprise administrator. It draws on every earlier phase — the engineer's respect for what systems can and can't do, the PM's habit of starting from a real workflow, and the platform instinct that capabilities beat features.

## What Regulation Teaches Product Managers

1. **Compliance is a design input, not a gate.** Bringing compliance and legal in at the sketch stage removes rework later.
2. **Partner integrations are where the time goes.** The custodian API integration is what moved setup from 31 days to 7, and choosing a custodian is a strategy decision, not a vendor swap.
3. **Start as an engineer if you can.** You will know when a simple request is actually a platform rewrite.
4. **Invest in internal tools early.** Self-service tooling for operations saves far more engineering time than it costs.
5. **Unify before you multiply.** Adding products to a fragmented platform creates exponential complexity.
6. **Measure adoption, not just deployment.** A feature that ships but is not used is a maintenance burden with no value.
